The doctor held him up, all small and red, cryin’ out like he already knew the world was too big and too loud for

moment, I felt somethin' shift inside me. It was like my heart stretched, makin' room for somethin' bigger than

all faded. There was only him.
When they placed him in my arms, he felt light as air but heavy with meaning. His skin was soft, so soft I was
afraid I might break him, but I held him tight, like I’d never let go. His tiny fingers curled and uncurled, and his
face—wrinkled like an old man’s—slowly relaxed as he settled into my arms. I looked down at him, and for the
first time, I understood what people meant when they talked about joy. The kind that runs deep, that makes
you feel like you could burst but still hold it all together.
Nadine looked at me, her face pale but glowin', her eyes tired but full of love. “He’s ours,” she whispered, her
voice barely a breath, but those words? They filled the room. “He’s ours.”
I nodded, feelin’ my throat tighten, too choked up to speak. For a moment, everything was perfect. Just me, my
son, and Nadine. The whole world, right there in that small hospital room. Nothing else mattered.

It wasn’t long after we brought our boy home that I noticed somethin' was off with Nadine. At first, I told myself
it was just the exhaustion that comes with bein' a new mom. Everyone says it’s hard, right? But this was
somethin’ deeper. She stopped smilin’. Her laugh, the one that used to fill up the whole room, got quiet, like it
was trapped somewhere far away. And then she stopped talkin' altogether.
Some days, she wouldn’t even get out of bed. I’d try to help, try to make things easier for her. I’d take care of the
baby, make the meals, clean the house. I’d sit by her, hold her hand, and talk to her about everything and
nothin’, hopin' she’d come back to me. But every time I looked in her eyes, it was like she was driftin' further and
further away. She wasn’t my Nadine anymore. She was there, but she wasn’t. Like she was locked inside herself,
and no matter how hard I tried, I couldn’t reach her.
They called it postpartum depression. Made it sound like somethin' with a simple fix, somethin' clinical. But it
wasn’t like that. It was like a weight had fallen over her, so heavy that she couldn’t lift it, and I couldn’t help.
Every day, I’d hope it would get better, that one morning she’d wake up, smile at me, and tell me it was all a bad
dream. But every day, she seemed to slip further.
Then one day, she didn’t get up at all. She just lay there, starin' at the ceiling, like the light comin’ through the
window meant nothin'. I remember holdin' our boy, rockin' him to sleep while my heart felt like it was splinterin'

That’s when the state stepped in. They said they’d been keepin' an eye on things, that it wasn’t safe for the baby.
Said Nadine wasn’t fit to care for him, and as for me, well, my record spoke for itself.
See, I’d made some mistakes back in the day. Got into trouble when I was younger, like a lot of us do. But I’d
turned it around, cleaned up my act. Still, that “scratch” on my record was enough for them. Didn’t matter how
far I’d come, didn’t matter how hard I’d fought to be the man I was. To them, I was just another mark on a file.

told them I’d do anything, anything, to keep my son. But their faces were stone, unmoved.
I watched from the window as they put him in the backseat of that car. My boy—my whole world—drivin' away
