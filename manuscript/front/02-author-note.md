## A note on the text

This book brings remembered experience into relation with documents, measurements, other people's accounts, and the questions those sources leave open. They do not all establish the same things. Remembered speech is not a transcript. A quotation from a document establishes what that document says; it does not, by itself, establish everything the document asserts. My account of another person's observation remains my account until that person's own contribution is separately available.

The date of an event, the date it returns in memory, the date an account is written, and the date someone receives it may differ. So may the order in which I can tell them. Where those differences affect the inquiry, they belong beside the passage concerned. A gap in recollection will not be completed merely because a continuous scene would be easier to read. An allegation will not be treated as an adjudication, or testimony as fiction because it remains disputed.

I have used artificial-intelligence tools substantially in research assistance, organization, drafting, and revision. They are not witnesses. Their fluency does not authenticate a source or settle an argument. I remain responsible for the language I accept, including the work of correcting it. Nor should the fluency of the written text, or of a voice reading it, be mistaken for an account of how readily I can speak.

The medical and legal questions concern particular events and circumstances. These pages are not instructions for another person's treatment or a substitute for independent investigation and judgment.

**Reader advisory.** The book includes accounts of childhood sexual violence, attempted suicide, medical trauma, disability, and death. You may pause, pass over a section, or return to it later. The invitation to read is not a demand to endure.
