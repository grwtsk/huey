## What changes in the book

The letter does not merely add a hopeful last paragraph. It changes the object of attention throughout Huey. A family has a life while documents are being written, while institutions deliberate, and while the book remains unfinished. An honest record matters partly because it should not consume the people whose lives it records. A usable channel matters partly because a person has something to say other than a complaint. A hand may write a correction, make something, touch another person or rest. A day can contain duties without belonging entirely to them.

These are editorial inferences from the supplied letter and the existing manuscript, not claims that the family reported additional events. The concrete source remains modest: lists, study, friends, family, two dogs, choir, younger children, youth activities, baking and a mother's happiness.

The nine qualities should be visible through those particulars and through the work's conduct. Do not turn them into nine newly invented episodes or nine mandatory chapter headings. Honesty and joy were expressly named without a separate anecdote; leave that limit intact.

## Exact candidate wording for later author review

The following short units are newly composed editorial candidates. They do not represent new memories, spoken dialogue or approved narrator testimony. Do not place them in canonical reading order without the existing review and identity/source reconciliation. They are not intended to be repeated verbatim in several places.

### MBD-C01 — receiving the mother's account

Rahel describes Lia making lists for the day. There is schoolwork to finish, and there is time she wants to spend with her friends, our family and the two dogs. Her mother is proud of the effort she puts into both. I want that part of the account to remain ordinary. The list is not a diagnosis, and the day is not a demonstration. It is a day Lia has things to do with.

### MBD-C02 — something made for another person

Lia bakes for her friends. Her mother tells me that they count on her for their birthday cakes. There is an occasion that belongs to someone else, and something Lia enjoys doing becomes part of it. I do not need to ask the cake to explain the book. I need to leave room in the book for the cake.

### MBD-C03 — a voice beside other voices

At Saint George, Rahel looks forward to hearing Lia sing with the choir. Her account begins with the happiness of being her mother. I have asked for my wife's observations to be heard in other rooms. Here she is telling us about our daughter. Those are her words to give, and they are not merely another way of speaking about me.

### MBD-C04 — assistance and ownership of meaning

The letter says that artificial intelligence helped organize and express her thoughts. The examples came from her. The assistance should remain visible without displacing the person who supplied them. A finished sentence can carry more than one kind of work; that does not make everyone who helped produce it a witness to what it says.

### MBD-C05 — what a response should leave possible

A usable response has a place in a day, and the day has other claims on it. There may be work to finish, someone to spend time with, music to hear, or a need to rest. I do not want every part of life to become material for a better account of what went wrong. I want an account accurate enough that the work of correcting it does not have to occupy everything else.

### MBD-C06 — a future not assigned by the book

Rahel wishes Lia many beautiful days ahead. She writes about studies that engage her mind, friendships in which she can be herself, and choices she has yet to make. I do not know what all those days will contain. They are not the conclusion of my case or a task I can assign her. Her mother is looking forward to knowing more of the person she will become.
