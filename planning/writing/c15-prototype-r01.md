# A Different Last Page

A letter can be written toward an answer that does not yet exist.

That is one of the strange things about correspondence. A person can prepare the place where another person's words might someday go. The date can be left blank. A field can wait for a receipt that has not happened. A question can be complete while its answer remains somewhere in the future.

I have such a document.

Its status matters to me now because I have spent so much of this book asking what happens when status is allowed to blur. Written is not sent. Sent is not received. Received is not read. Read is not understood. Understood is not agreed. A proposal is not an action merely because the sentence is ready for one.

The document has places where I do not know what the future will contain.

I have learned to leave those places empty.

That is not passivity.

It is fidelity to time.

If I fill the blank with what I expect another person to say, I have converted anticipation into evidence. If I write an answer before the person has answered, I have done in prose what I have objected to in records: allowed one voice to occupy the place where another person's account belongs.

So the blank remains.

There are thirty-two themes in the working map I made from that document. I paired them because each institutional question eventually produced another question that belonged to me.

What happened?

What did you receive?

What did you review?

Who decided?

What authority did that person have?

What did the referral accomplish rather than merely initiate?

What did the record preserve?

What did it collapse?

What did the notice actually tell me?

What did I understand?

What remained possible after the answer?

And then, beneath each of those questions, another one began to appear.

What do I do with an answer if it arrives?

What do I do with the absence of one?

What do I owe a person whose account contradicts mine?

What do I owe the reader if the evidence does not resolve the thing as cleanly as anger would like it to?

What does correction mean if correction comes too late to return the life consumed while waiting for it?

What does trust mean after trust has become an object of proof?

I had begun by asking institutions for answers.

I ended by discovering that the manuscript was also asking me for one.

***

A medical record has a way of looking complete even when it is not.

The sentences line up.

The dates arrive in order.

The headings repeat.

A name appears beside an observation and the observation acquires the authority of placement.

The page does not show the pause before the sentence was written.

It does not show what was not asked.

It does not show the patient trying to interrupt.

It does not show the witness whose account was not yet taken.

It does not show the doctor deciding which fact deserved to become durable.

It does not show what another doctor later read first.

The record is not false merely because it is incomplete.

But incompleteness has direction.

What survives has an advantage over what does not.

That is one reason I have spent so much time on provenance.

Who said this?

Who saw it?

Who copied it?

Who inferred it?

Who disputed it?

Who corrected it?

Who never had the opportunity?

If the book cannot preserve those distinctions, then I have no right to demand that medicine preserve them for me.

That is the first place the last page becomes different.

The last page cannot simply turn the moral burden outward.

It has to return.

***

I have used words in this book that are larger than any one sentence.

*Abuse.*

*Assault.*

*Battery.*

*Abandonment.*

*Trauma.*

*Trust.*

*Crime.*

*Racism.*

*Violence.*

*Love.*

*Mercy.*

*Justice.*

Some of the heaviest words I know travel with surprisingly small endings.

*Racism. Racist.*

*Socialism. Socialist.*

*Communism. Communist.*

*Fascism. Fascist.*

*Capitalism. Capitalist.*

*Ableism. Ableist.*

The suffix is tiny.

The burden is not.

A few letters can move language from an act to a pattern, from a pattern to a system, from a system to a person, and from a person to what sounds very much like a verdict.

Sometimes that movement is justified.

Sometimes history would be falsified by refusing the word.

Sometimes a soft synonym is not fairness but concealment.

But the opposite error is available too.

A grave word can arrive so quickly that the relations it is supposed to describe never catch up.

That is the danger I have tried to describe in the medical record.

The inherited sentence is already loaded.

The patient's correction still has to travel.

Language can behave the same way.

The label is cached.

The person is not.

I do not want to solve that problem by making the words unavailable.

I want the opposite.

I want language strong enough to name what is real.

I want *racism* if the relations support racism.

I want *assault* if the facts support assault.

I want *communist*, *socialist*, *fascist*, *capitalist*, *ableist*, *racist* and every other serious word to remain available to the future.

But I want their provenance attached.

What definition?

What act?

What relation?

What evidence?

Whose description?

What remains disputed?

What changed when the word was applied?

The suffix cannot do that work for us.

Similarity of sound cannot do it either.

*Community.*

*Communion.*

*Communal.*

*Communism.*

Words can grow beside one another without becoming the same institution.

A movement can place a word inside its name without acquiring every meaning that word has carried elsewhere.

A political program can call itself social without resolving the economics by the adjective alone.

A church can speak of communion without becoming a theory of the state.

The word tells us where inquiry may begin.

It does not relieve us of inquiry.

***

There is a temptation in confession.

The speaker names something ugly about himself and the naming begins to look like proof that he has escaped it.

I do not want that bargain.

To confess is not to become innocent by grammatical transformation.

The word *was* does not absolve the word *am*.

The word *am* does not determine every future act.

The sentence has to remain alive long enough to change what the speaker does next.

That is one reason the final confession in this chapter cannot be a performance of purity.

It cannot turn another person's love into a certificate.

It cannot use another person's suffering as scenery for my moral development.

It cannot require the reader to reassure me.

And it cannot make the act of naming myself more important than the people whose lives made the question unavoidable.

***

The same problem scales outward.

As I write, the President of the United States has argued that hostile political language directed toward him and his allies can be dangerous. In April 2026, President Donald Trump said that Democratic “hate speech” was dangerous for the country. [LP01]

That proposition deserves to be taken seriously on its own terms.

Political rhetoric can have consequences.

A person does not surrender the right to object to dehumanizing or dangerous language merely because he holds office.

The principle becomes useful only if it survives rotation.

In June 2026, Trump used the word “Dumocrats” and said Democrats were dumb. [LP02] During the 2026 campaign season he used *Communist* and *Socialist* against particular Democratic and progressive candidates; Reuters also reported that Republican campaigns themselves were divided over how useful that line of attack was. [LP03] In November 2025 he called six Democratic lawmakers “traitors,” described their conduct as seditious and wrote that it could be punishable by death; the White House subsequently said he was not seeking their execution. [LP04]

I do not need a political verdict about him for the principle to survive.

If hostile rhetoric used against a President can be consequential, language used by a President can be consequential too.

That is reciprocity.

The office does not change the dictionary.

It changes the reach.

A patient's word can disappear in a chart.

A doctor's word can become the chart.

A clinic's word can condition access.

A regulator's word can alter a license.

A court's word can become judgment.

A President's word can cross the country before the sentence has finished leaving his mouth.

The right to speak survives that amplification.

So should responsibility for what the amplified word carries.

***

The word *fight* makes the difficulty especially clear.

On January 6, 2021, Trump told supporters they had to “fight like hell.” In the same speech he told them to march “peacefully and patriotically” and make their voices heard. [LP05]

Both belong to the record.

I cannot remove one because the other is inconvenient.

Effect does not retroactively become dictionary definition.

Intent does not erase effect.

Context does not mean selecting whichever neighboring phrase protects the conclusion I already wanted.

The responsible inquiry is larger.

What was said?

By whom?

To whom?

Under what circumstances?

What meanings were reasonably available?

What did the speaker know?

What followed?

What relation between the words and the result can actually be supported?

And which of those questions belongs to moral judgment, which to history, which to law, and which remains unresolved?

I have asked medicine to preserve distinctions like these.

My severe sentence in an examination room was not a diagnosis merely because it was severe.

A physician's sentence does not become true merely because it entered the chart.

A notice does not become understood merely because the institution can prove that it was sent.

A metaphor does not become pathology because another person repeats it.

The office changes none of those rules.

The office changes the cost of getting them wrong.

That is why I think the burden of language should rise with entrusted power.

Not because powerful people should be permitted fewer words.

Because authority is an amplifier.

***

That principle reaches farther than politics.

A doctor speaks with delegated authority.

A clinic speaks with institutional authority.

A university speaks with historical and public authority.

A medical board speaks with regulatory authority.

A police officer, prosecutor, judge, legislature and President each speak from offices that can turn language into consequences unavailable to an ordinary speaker.

The powers differ.

So do the legal rules.

But one moral relation remains visible across them:

the farther a word can travel because of the office from which it is spoken, the greater the responsibility to know what the word is being asked to carry.

This does not make forceful language improper.

Sometimes forceful language is the only accurate language.

There are acts for which euphemism would be its own distortion.

A survivor should be able to say *rape*.

A witness should be able to say *racism*.

A citizen should be able to say *fascism*, *socialism*, *communism*, *capitalism*, *ableism*, *sexism* or any other consequential classification when the word is the one the evidence can responsibly bear.

I want satire.

I want anger.

I want the four-letter word when four letters are enough.

Freedom of language is not protected by making language harmless.

It is protected by refusing to let power decide that only certain speakers deserve context.

***

This is where the argument becomes uncomfortable for me.

I have complained that medicine can turn a patient's language into evidence against him.

I have complained that a phrase can be detached from the room in which it was spoken, stripped of trauma, stripped of disability, stripped of the time it took to say it, and then carried forward as though the sentence explained the person.

If I believe that, then I cannot do the same thing merely because the target of my sentence possesses more power than I do.

I cannot demand provenance from a doctor while pretending my interpretation needs none.

I cannot ask an institution to preserve context and then remove context from language spoken by someone I oppose.

I cannot demand that a regulator distinguish allegation from finding while writing an allegation as though saying it completed the investigation.

I cannot object when metaphor becomes pathology in my record and then turn my own grotesque metaphor about another person into a diagnosis.

The discipline has to survive contact with me.

That is the confession at the societal scale.

***

There is another danger.

Fear of misuse can itself become a reason to impoverish language.

That is not what I want.

I do not want a future so frightened of the word *racist* that racism cannot be named.

I do not want a future so frightened of the word *assault* that the injured person must translate violence into administrative vocabulary before anyone will listen.

I do not want difficult classifications replaced by polite fog.

The rule is not:

never use the grave word.

The rule is:

carry what makes the grave word responsible.

A supported grave word should not be weakened merely because it embarrasses power.

An unsupported grave word should not be strengthened merely because it humiliates an opponent.

Evidence may sustain the word.

Evidence may narrow it.

Evidence may contradict it.

Sometimes evidence may remain incomplete while the first-person account remains faithfully attributable to the person who lived it.

Those are different states.

The language should show the difference.

That is not censorship.

It is custody.

***

I have spent years learning how quickly a word can outlive the event that produced it.

A note is copied.

A phrase becomes shorthand.

Shorthand becomes inheritance.

The inheritance becomes the starting point for a later judgment.

Eventually the word appears to have no author because everyone has received it from somewhere else.

The book cannot condemn that mechanism while reproducing it.

So when I write *racism*, the word should be able to point backward.

When I write *crime*, the word should be able to point backward.

When I write *trust*, *mercy*, *justice*, *abandonment*, *love*, *violence*, each should point toward the relations, evidence, memory, faith, law or metaphor from which I am asking it to draw meaning.

And when the source is me, the arrow must say so.

That does not make my testimony worthless.

It makes the testimony mine.

***

There is a difference between confession and surrender.

I do not confess in order to make every contested claim disappear.

I do not confess in order to create a symmetry in which everyone has harmed everyone equally and therefore nobody has to answer for anything.

I do not confess in order to make the reader admire my willingness to confess.

I confess because the method fails if it stops at the author's skin.

The person writing the record has power over the record.

The person arranging the scenes has power over sequence.

The person deciding which quotation remains has power over emphasis.

The person naming a chapter has power over what the reader expects before the first sentence arrives.

I have that power here.

So I inherit responsibility for it.

Confession does not absolve the speaker from custody.

If anything, confession begins it.

***

That responsibility is social because language never remains entirely private once it enters a common field.

A doctor's note may reach another doctor.

A newspaper headline may reach millions.

A political slogan may survive the policy it once described.

A diagnosis may become an insurance code.

An accusation may become a search result.

A confession may become a quotation detached from the chapter that gave it meaning.

The future reader may know none of us.

That reader may know only the word.

This is why I do not want the words ending in *-ism* and *-ist* to arrive at the last page alone.

I want the people attached.

I want the acts attached.

I want the record attached.

I want the uncertainty attached.

I want the power attached.

I want the possibility of correction attached.

I want the right to use the word preserved by the obligation to carry what makes the word true.

That is the social responsibility of confession.

The right to speak and the duty to answer for speech should rise together.

And where speech is joined to entrusted authority, the duty should become heavier, not lighter.

***

There is still a blank in the document I began with.

There are several.

Perhaps some will eventually receive dates.

Perhaps some will receive names.

Perhaps one will contain an answer that changes something I have written here.

If that happens, the book should change where the evidence requires it.

That possibility is not weakness either.

It is the difference between authorship and ownership of truth.

I can own these sentences.

I cannot own everything they are trying to describe.

Other people remain capable of answering.

Records remain capable of contradicting me.

A witness remains capable of remembering differently.

An institution remains capable of producing evidence I have not seen.

A reader remains capable of refusing the relation I thought the page made visible.

That is the last responsibility I can give the language.

Not to win after I have stopped speaking.

To remain answerable.

I have asked other people to bear that weight.

Now I have to leave my own words where you can examine them.
