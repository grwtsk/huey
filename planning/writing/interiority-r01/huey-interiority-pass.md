# Huey — inner life and medical trauma

Editorial pass prepared September 22, 2026 for R.A. Jacob Martone.

## What needed changing

The material does not lack an inner life. Its difficulty is that, at important moments, the narration steps outside that life to explain why the reader should accept its presence. An experience is stated, then qualified, then defended against a possible misreading, then protected against another. Some qualifications are essential. Others begin to reproduce on the page the examination the narrator has already had to undergo.

This pass separates those functions. An unknown cause stays unknown. Another person's thoughts are not supplied. A recalled conversation is not converted into a recording. But the narrator need not demonstrate, after every felt experience, that he knows the rules of evidence. Those distinctions should support the account without repeatedly replacing it.

The strongest existing image is the hand. It once approached the car door; another person's strength removed the door. It lifted a child. It resembles the mother's hand. It now negotiates with a keyboard. These relations give the reader more than a claim about retained personhood: they allow the same person to remain present across different capacities. I have left the later passages intact rather than turn each return of the hand into another explanation of what it symbolizes.

## Time, rather than a catalogue of symptoms

The first pressure is the interval between what happens, what can be understood or chosen, and what another person receives. In the encounter account, awareness can remain while effective interruption becomes difficult. In the telephone account, words can already be formed while their delivery fails to convey the ease with which they are known. In the accommodation account, additional time can also be needed for understanding and consolidation. These are related but different experiences. A single image of a complete answer trapped behind slow speech would misrepresent their range.

The proposed Chapter 3 passage remains with that interval. It does not add a new touch, a new utterance, a sensory flashback or another person's intention. The chapter's direct nonconsent statement remains unchanged. The surrounding uncertainty about the biological explanation of the bodily change also remains. The prose can convey helplessness without converting its vividness into proof of someone else's knowledge or a mechanism of injury.

## The page does not preserve the cost of reaching it

The second pressure is the difference between producing testimony and receiving it. A finished page supplies its sentences in order. It does not display every stopped attempt, the need to return, or the physical effort of continuing. In the already admitted chapter, the revision keeps the wrist and the hand visible beneath that smoothness.

The proposed Chapter 12 passage draws on the author's supplied statement that writing the account can bring back seeing, hearing and feeling, rather than merely an abstract recollection. It does not manufacture the contents of those sensations. The sentence stays close to what has actually been supplied. Writing remains a means of access, but not an escape from the experience being written about.

That distinction also changes the treatment of an unfinished account. The previous version observed that stopping did not make the unfinished paragraph adequate. The revision does not ask the narrator to acknowledge that charge against himself. It shows the unfinished sentence waiting, with some of the cost already incurred. The absence of a completed record is not narrated as the absence of effort.

## The maker is not confined to the past

The earlier Chapter 2 gives the maker a life before the encounter. The private proposal makes room for his continuation. It draws on the author's supplied introspection about long-term planning: remaining with thought can reveal an ongoing design before a sentence is ready to describe it. This is proposed literary expression of that personal account, not a general scientific finding.

Research, music, writing and making should not become admission tests for dignity. Nor should they appear solely as possessions that trauma took away. The narrator is making this book. His capacity to wonder, revise and become absorbed in something is part of the present tense of the work.

The final proposal makes a small but material correction: instead of asking what a “corrected life” would be for, the prose asks what life beyond the work of correction might hold. The record can need correction without the life being an erroneous entry.

## What remains intact

Affection and accountability do not need to cancel one another. The admitted chapter's account of the earlier physician's arrangements and apology remains. Her child remains a birth. The reader is not asked to erase that tenderness to understand a later absence of care. Equally, tenderness is not used to dissolve the practical consequence.

The name passages, family recognitions, welcome, humor, attraction and later return to love in the checked-in chapter are unchanged. The book's protected closing is neither reconstructed nor revised. This pass does not claim that joy cures trauma or makes investigation unnecessary. It leaves joy a part of the life rather than an outcome awarded for settling the case.

## Scope and source status

Eight edits have been made to the current, publicly admitted chapter, using only material already present there. Eight additional replacements are proposed for Chapters 2, 3, 7 and 12 of an older extracted reading copy. Those private proposals are not installed into the latest full manuscript: that complete version was not retrievable in this pass. The readable proposals and exact before/after data are included here so that they can be reconciled without overwriting later work.

The private writing draws on the retrieved earlier chapter passages; the author's September 3 project conversation about long-term planning; the supplied written-first accommodation request; the author-authored record-entry statement's description of the cost of testimony; and the author-authored encounter account distinguishing awareness, action and consent. These are separately attributable personal sources, not independently authenticated clinical findings. The source register in `earlier-chapter-proposals.json` records the basis for each proposal.

This is an editorial draft for the author's judgment. It does not request a retelling of the experiences. No full-book completion, independent factual adjudication, performed narration, measured runtime or publication clearance is claimed.

# Proposed earlier-chapter revisions

These are isolated replacements in an older extracted reading copy, not a replacement for the latest manuscript. All new wording is proposed literary adaptation for author review. The private source material and these passages have not been uploaded to GitHub.

## I01 — 2. A Life Already Under Way

I have supplied examples of my research, art, and reasoning when trying to explain the difference between what I can think and what I can rapidly express. Those examples also belong to a life in which making things had purposes of its own. I could become absorbed in work I had chosen, give time to something because I wanted it to exist, and discover what I was doing by staying with it.

The maker was already there. He is not only there, in the earlier part of the story. Thought continues beyond what I can immediately report; sometimes, by remaining with it, I become aware of a design that has been continuing without a sentence ready to describe it. The work is still mine. It is not a qualification for care. It is one of the things I want care to leave me free to do.

*Editorial change:* Keep the maker alive in the present, not merely as a lost credential. Render the author’s supplied long-planning introspection as proposed first-person reflection, not a universal theory of unconscious cognition.

## I02 — 3. The Room

My sentence was severe because the experience I was trying to report was severe. It was not a diagnosis. It was what I could say of what was happening to me, and those words belonged to the room before they belonged to anyone else's explanation.

*Editorial change:* Retain the difference between a report and a diagnosis without staging an imagined temptation to falsify the account. This does not change the surrounding causal qualifications.

## I03 — 3. The Room

The difficulty was no longer only in finding words for pain. I was conscious of what was happening, but I could not quickly turn that awareness into speech or make it interrupt the encounter. The room continued while I was still trying to find a deliberate way to act within it.

I had known that separation during earlier trauma. I do not have to bring those scenes into this one to recognize it: an event can reach me before my response can reach anyone else. I remain there through the interval. Knowing what is happening does not, by itself, give me the means to stop it.

I was one person, caught in that difficulty. Automatic behavior could come before I recovered deliberate control. What might be visible from outside and what I was able to choose were not the same account.

*Editorial change:* Render temporal asymmetry from inside the reported experience. Do not split the narrator into a perfect internal mind and a merely defective body, or invent additional actions or dialogue.

## I04 — 3. The Room

This is how I have described January 24: hostility first, then impaired deliberate control, then unwanted physical contact before I had recovered enough expressive agency to interrupt effectively. That is the sequence I report, not a rule that distress automatically removes consent from every medical encounter.

*Editorial change:* Keep the case-specific allegation and the substantive limit intact while reducing repeated defensive explanation. The following direct statement of nonconsent remains unchanged.

## I05 — 7. A Page of My Own

The offer of more time also needs to meet the difficulty that is actually present. Sometimes the answer has formed and my speech cannot carry it quickly enough. At other times I need to understand the question, remain with it, and let an answer take shape. A question I can reread stays with me through that work. It does not have to be recovered from a conversation that has already moved on.

*Editorial change:* Distinguish difficulty delivering a formed answer from the need for deliberation and consolidation. Avoid implying that every delayed response conceals a fully formed answer.

## I06 — 12. The Human Cost

I have no complete time sheet for those pages. I have accounts of what the writing required from me: finding the source, checking the date, separating a request from an action, returning to a sentence until it says neither more nor less than I can support. The absence of a measured total leaves the hours uncounted. It does not give them back.

*Editorial change:* Keep the absence of measured duration explicit without making the narrator rehearse an invented intention to falsify a time sheet.

## I07 — 12. The Human Cost

There is also the return to what the sentence describes. In my earlier record-entry statement, I wrote that I did not merely remember it when I tried to give the account. I saw it, heard it, felt it. I was trying to make a record of the experience, and the work of making it brought the experience near again. Writing gave me a way to say what speech had not carried. It did not place me beyond the reach of what I was saying.

When I have to stop, the stopping leaves no paragraph. The unfinished sentence remains where I left it, though I have already spent something of myself reaching that point. To continue, I must come back to it. That return belongs to the cost of the account, even when the finished page gives the reader no reason to pause.

*Editorial change:* Use the author’s already supplied sensory account without inventing a new flashback scene. Make the limits of writing-as-access visible without declaring writing either inherently healing or inherently harmful.

## I08 — 12. The Human Cost

The page in front of the reader is one use of that desire. Unlike a request for correction, it can remain with a thought long enough to ask what life beyond the work of correction might hold. It can return to family, making, worship or rest without offering them as exhibits. Their place in my life did not originate in the need to prove that something had been lost.

*Editorial change:* Correct the object of correction: the record may need repair; the life is not an erroneous entry. Keep the existing return to ordinary purposes.
