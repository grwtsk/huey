# A standard of reception

R.A. Jacob Martone — proposed passage for the existing instruments discussion.

> [!WARNING]
> **WORKING DRAFT — CLAIM VERIFICATION INCOMPLETE.** This proposed passage draws from the author-attributed source works identified below. It is not an independent clinical assessment, finding of wrongdoing, or accepted manuscript insertion.

I have used more than words to communicate.

The source collection contains a painting, *I BLED SO*, presented as a reaction painting that gives visible form to pain. It also contains a composition, *Discipline And Punish*, from *Violence*, which I described as a response to my experience of January 24, 2019. These works belong in the account of how I have tried to make experience available to another person. They should remain identifiable as the kinds of expression they are. [1][2]

A painting is not a neurological examination. A composition does not decide what happened in an examination room. Neither supplies, by itself, the clinical explanation I have requested.

But neither is nothing.

There is a difference between asking an image to prove the cause of pain and recognizing that the person who made it is communicating pain. The first requires evidence the image cannot supply on its own. The second permits a question: what are you trying to tell me?

That is the standard of reception I want to place beside the standard of expression.

I have spent effort constructing forms through which an experience might become more legible. The recipient need not adopt my interpretation to receive the communication. The recipient can ask what the image carries, what the words mean, where the account can be made more precise, and which part requires a different kind of inquiry.

Those questions do not diminish the work. They keep it from being required to perform work it was never capable of performing.

The same discipline applies to a quotation.

When I write that dignity, rather than authority, should become the measure of progress, I am proposing a criterion for judging conduct. I am not presenting a laboratory measurement. When I write that a failing voice is not an absent mind, I am resisting the substitution of an outward channel for the whole person. I am not claiming that a sentence can decide every question about cognition or capacity. The words have force without being made to guarantee more than they say. [3][4]

That distinction belongs to the institution as well as to me.

An institution's description of itself should remain distinguishable from the conduct by which the description could be tested. A promise of access is not the experience of using it. A reference to verified sources is not the examination of those sources. A published statement of care still has to meet the person who seeks care.

I do not ask that artistic expression displace clinical judgment. I ask that judgment identify what it is judging.

Is it considering the person's report? Is it considering the proposed cause? Is it evaluating a particular clinical finding? Or has it begun to evaluate whether the person has expressed suffering in a sufficiently familiar form?

The last question can consume the others without answering them.

A standard of reception would keep the distinction visible. It would not require every expression to become an examination, every recollection to become a recording, or every appeal for attention to become proof of liability. It would ask what each contribution can support and what further work is needed.

It would also resist turning the contribution into an obligation.

I should not have to make a painting, compose music, master evidentiary distinctions, or prepare a book before my report deserves careful attention. A person who has none of these means has not supplied a lesser reason for care.

The work I have made is part of how I have spoken. It is not a professional service the institution may require me to provide in place of its own.

**I have worked to make myself understood. The standard must also examine what was done to understand me.**
