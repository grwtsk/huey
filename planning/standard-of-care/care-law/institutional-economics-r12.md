# HUEY-CL12 — institutional economies of trust and responsibility

**Huey book work.** Institutional verification [#183](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/183); jurisdiction [#184](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/184); reciprocal notice [#324](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/324) / [#325](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/325); institutional economies [#327](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/327); continuing development [PR #122](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/pull/122).

> Working draft. This matrix identifies published authority, institutional roles, routing possibilities, limits and economic questions. It does not establish that a particular office received a complaint, accepted responsibility, violated a duty, incurred a modeled cost, or owes a remedy.

## Source-grounded starting point

The September 17 patient response expressly separates current neurological care from investigation of alleged wrongdoing. It asks the investigation to follow the actual decisions rather than predetermine whether responsibility rests with an individual clinician, clinic leadership, a cross-clinic process or a broader UCSF system. It also asks each recipient to act only within actual authority and to route matters outside that authority rather than assume a power the recipient does not possess.

The same source describes a possible burden-propagation sequence: unresolved access, predictability, continuity, consent, attribution and communication can become incomplete history, disputed notes, correction work, continuity disputes and repeated protective correspondence. That is attributed testimony and analysis. This matrix does not convert each proposed arrow into a proved causal chain.

## Governing distinction

The four levels below are **not interchangeable**:

1. **UCSF / UCSF Health** — direct care, patient-access functions, records, complaints and local institutional action where policy assigns them.
2. **University of California** — system governance and delegated financial/organizational authority; not automatically an individual patient-grievance office.
3. **Medical Board of California** — physician licensure, complaint investigation and professional discipline within statutory jurisdiction; not a treating service or damages forum.
4. **California's distributed public architecture** — separate civil-rights, facility-regulation, court and other governmental functions; not one central care coordinator.

Affiliation does not itself create responsibility. A function is assigned only where a source supports the authority.

## Matrix

### 1. UCSF / UCSF Health

**Published authority.** UCSF Interim Policy 700-15 assigns responsibility for determining, implementing and maintaining accommodations for UCSF Health patients and companions to the treating clinical department or inpatient unit and/or Patient Relations. It also provides financial-escalation paths for accommodations and barrier removal. UCSF's patient-rights materials state that patients may complain without compromising quality or future access to care and may expect a reasonable and timely response. Patient Relations identifies itself as a route for unresolved concerns and lists external agencies. UCSF's accessibility page states that reasonable accommodations and auxiliary aids/services are available without charge and directs patients to their care team or Patient Relations.

**What this level can plausibly own.**
- the clinical question and clinical continuity;
- accommodation assessment within the actual care setting;
- patient-relations intake and grievance handling;
- accessible routing to the responsible local function;
- records/privacy/patient-safety work through the corresponding UCSF functions;
- nonprivileged identification of which office accepted which task.

**What this level does not become merely because a complaint is serious.**
- a criminal court;
- a prosecutor;
- the Medical Board;
- a civil damages tribunal;
- a guarantee that every requested mechanism must be provided exactly as requested.

**Economic question.** When a local unit declines or cannot perform a task, did the task disappear, move to another UCSF function, or move to the patient/support person? A local workload reduction is not yet evidence of lower total work.

**Lowest-burden repair to test.** One accepted coordinator can identify the clinical owner, access owner, records owner and any genuinely external question. The coordinator need not personally perform each task.

**Unresolved factual work.** The public policies establish routes and responsibilities, but not who actually reviewed the September materials, what internal tasks were accepted, whether any case identifiers were created, or whether current policy 1.01.10 governed each disputed event.

### 2. University of California

**Published authority.** Regents Bylaw 22 places University governance authority in the Board of Regents and delegates University operations to the President subject to reserved powers. Bylaw 31 makes campus Chancellors the executive heads of their campuses and assigns campus organization, internal administration, operation and financial management within Regents/Presidential policy and budget. Bylaw 24 gives the Health Services Committee strategic direction and oversight concerning UC Health. BUS-10 governs financial-transaction accountability and states, among other things, that reporting lines and communication channels must be clear, accountability cannot be delegated, and delegated tasks must go to people qualified and authorized to perform them.

**What this level can plausibly own.**
- system and campus governance where authority has actually been delegated;
- financial/accountability structures under applicable UC policy;
- strategic oversight of UC Health at the level reserved or delegated to Regents/system leadership;
- escalation where a campus policy expressly invokes central resources.

**What this level does not become merely because UCSF is part of UC.**
- the treating neurologist;
- the ordinary first recipient of a UCSF patient grievance;
- the Medical Board;
- a source of automatic UC-wide liability from one campus event.

**Economic question.** Does system scale reduce coordination costs through clear delegation and specialized functions, or does an actual boundary leave the patient or clinician to recreate the missing connection? BUS-10 supports a financial-accountability principle; it is not a general clinical-duty statute.

**Lowest-burden repair to test.** Identify the exact delegated authority before escalating upward. If the matter remains campus-level, keep it there. If policy or governance genuinely assigns a system-level role, identify the responsible function and the accepted handoff.

**Unresolved factual work.** No current evidence in this packet establishes that the Regents, UC President, Health Services Committee or another UCOP function received or controlled the patient-specific decisions at issue.

### 3. Medical Board of California

**Published authority.** The Board states that it licenses and regulates physicians, investigates complaints within its jurisdiction and disciplines licensees when violations are proved through its process. Quality-of-care and office-practice complaints can fall within that work. Complaints may be screened, investigated, closed, cited/fined or referred to the Attorney General for formal disciplinary proceedings. The Board also states that it cannot provide medical care or financial compensation for malpractice.

**What this level can plausibly own.**
- jurisdictional screening of a complaint about an individual physician;
- evidence collection and medical-consultant review where applicable;
- professional discipline under the Medical Practice Act when the evidentiary and procedural standards are satisfied;
- a disposition that distinguishes insufficient evidence, no apparent violation, lesser enforcement and formal accusation.

**What this level does not own.**
- ongoing neurological care;
- UCSF accommodation implementation;
- UCSF records routing;
- civil compensation;
- criminal adjudication merely because a complaint alleges criminal conduct.

**Economic question.** Professional regulation can reduce information asymmetry and repeat-risk externalities when jurisdiction and evidence support action, but investigation itself consumes public, professional and patient time. Neither under-enforcement nor over-enforcement is costless.

**Lowest-burden repair to test.** Submit only the evidence and issue set needed for the Board's actual jurisdiction; preserve clinical-access work in parallel rather than making regulatory completion a prerequisite to care.

**Unresolved factual work.** This matrix does not determine the status, merits, timeliness or outcome of any particular Medical Board complaint.

### 4. California's distributed public architecture

**Published authority.** California does not present one agency that centrally manages all health-care disputes. The Civil Rights Department says it enforces civil-rights laws concerning state-funded programs and investigates covered discrimination complaints; its state-funded-program materials include disability and reasonable-accommodation/access issues. The Department of Public Health's Center for Health Care Quality regulates licensed/certified health-care facilities within its purview and investigates facility complaints. Separate professional, civil, criminal and judicial functions remain governed by their own law and jurisdiction.

**What this level can plausibly own, through the correct agency or forum.**
- CRD: covered disability/civil-rights complaints within the laws it enforces;
- CDPH/CHCQ: facility compliance and complaints within its licensing/certification jurisdiction;
- Medical Board: physician professional regulation, treated separately above;
- courts, prosecutors and law enforcement: only the matters assigned by applicable law and procedure.

**What "California" does not mean.**
- one office has all of these powers;
- a complaint to one agency automatically gives notice to every other agency;
- a state-law route establishes federal-law coverage or vice versa;
- jurisdictional fragmentation itself proves illegality.

**Economic question.** A sequence of individually correct "not our function" responses can still create transaction cost if the person must identify and bridge every jurisdictional boundary. That observation supports a design question, not a conclusion that California has a legal duty to centrally coordinate an individual case.

**Lowest-burden repair to test.** For each unresolved proposition identify the correct forum once, preserve the routing evidence and stop re-litigating the jurisdiction question in every later communication.

**Unresolved factual work.** Coverage, limitations periods, exhaustion rules, remedies and event-date law remain proposition-specific work under #184. This matrix does not calculate a deadline or choose a forum for litigation.

## Reciprocal notice across the four levels

The notice-state ladder from #324/#325 is useful precisely because the institutions differ.

For any consequential communication, keep these states separate where evidence permits:

`authored → recorded → routed → delivered/available → received → accessible → reviewed → understood/opportunity → acknowledged → agreed/authorized`

The strongest supported state should be recorded. A later state is not inferred merely because it favors the sender.

At UCSF, a patient-authored document in an EHR does not by itself establish which clinician read or understood it; likewise an institution-authored portal item does not by itself establish patient understanding or agreement absent the governing notice rule and evidence. At UC, campus/system routing must be tied to actual delegated authority. At the Medical Board, complaint receipt is not a disciplinary finding. At CRD or CDPH, intake or jurisdictional acceptance is not a merits finding.

A different rule may be legitimate because a statute, regulation, agency relationship, policy or procedural rule says so. The difference should be identified rather than hidden inside the word "notice."

## Common-good proposition

The matrix supports a bounded proposition: **responsibility is cheaper to coordinate when its owner, limits and handoff are visible.** That is a design and stewardship claim. Whether a particular failure was avoidable, unlawful, professionally deficient or economically wasteful requires its own evidence.

A patient's September response asks for exactly this kind of traceability: responsible office, direct route, referral date, case/reference number where one exists, and the role receiving or deciding the matter. The request itself does not prove that such a review existed or that any office was legally required to create a case number.

It does **not** close #327, #183, #184, #324 or #325. Closure still requires patient-specific evidence of actual routing, acceptance, review, decisions, notice states and any system-level involvement.
