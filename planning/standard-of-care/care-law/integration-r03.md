## What changes inside the existing argument

Chapter 10 connects the institution's already-described tools to ordinary clinical listening before legal escalation. It develops communication and consent through the patient's supplied request, identifies current UCSF responsibility and resource routes, and gives concrete questions for examining a refusal and a failed handoff. Its existing public-history, favorable-account and source-scope distinctions remain.

Chapter 11 separates preserving correspondence from amendment and addendum procedures; develops the difference between evidence requested, received, evaluated and relied upon; and distinguishes clinical care, ethics advice, investigation and legal adjudication. It retains the patient's expressed confidence in the physician alongside the request to follow actual decisions. Before/during/after diligence and comparisons of transferred burdens now lead into the existing cost chapter.

The work retains source qualifications without importing repeated editor-origin self-rebukes into the narrator's voice. Current public rules are not represented as proof of what happened historically. An official source does not erase testimony; testimony does not manufacture an institutional action. Proposed low-burden alternatives are comparisons to test, not promised savings.
