## Show where damage can propagate—and where it can stop

I ask reviewers to trace possible downstream effects rather than treat the original encounter as the only event capable of examination. <!-- C115 | proposal | 185 | S00 -->

## Follow the decision until the patient reaches an answer

For every proposed handoff, identify the sending function, receiving function, question, lawful information scope, acceptance status, next step, and fallback if the handoff fails. <!-- C250 | proposal | 183 | S00 -->

In the circular institution I am trying to describe, every door can be open while every answer sends the patient back to the corridor. <!-- C255 | metaphor | 185 | S00 -->

## Trust, the common good, and the exit from the circle

By trust, I mean a relationship in which a person can discover what another has undertaken, what that person knows, what remains uncertain, and how an error will be answered. <!-- C127 | proposal | 185 | S00 -->
I ask that entrusted authority become visible in fidelity to those undertakings rather than in a demand that the patient stop asking how they were performed. <!-- C128 | proposal | 185 | S00 -->
For this argument, the common good includes care that other patients can use and standards that clinicians can follow without being required to conceal uncertainty or inherit an unsupported judgment. <!-- C129 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->
Any claimed benefit beyond this case must remain a proposal or be supported by evidence appropriate to its scale. <!-- C130 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->

My image of a Dantean recursive cul-de-sac is an original metaphor for responsibility sent in a circle, not a phrase I attribute to Dante or a sentence I pronounce upon another person's soul. <!-- C131 | metaphor | 185 | S00 -->
In that imagined circle, each office points to another until the patient returns to the first doorway carrying a thicker file and the same unanswered need. <!-- C132 | metaphor | 185 | S00 -->
The exit I ask for is concrete: one accepted responsibility, one accessible exchange, one accurately attributed statement, one competent review, and a route that ends in an answer rather than another circuit. <!-- C133 | proposal | 183 | S00 -->

I understand mercy here as restraint from unnecessary injury, not concealment of evidence. <!-- C134 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->
I understand courage as willingness to examine an unwelcome fact, not as a licence to accuse beyond the evidence. <!-- C135 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->
I understand fidelity as remaining answerable to an undertaking when its easiest procedural step has failed. <!-- C136 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->
I understand correction as a possibility that must remain open to every participant, including me. <!-- C137 | proposal | 185 | S00,S03 -->
No theological metaphor is offered as evidence of historical misconduct or as a substitute for the elements of a legal claim. <!-- C138 | proposal | 185 | S00 -->
