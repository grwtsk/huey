# World-first composition and argument cuts

Author: R.A. Jacob Martone  
Program: #396  
Architecture: #404  
Centerpiece flow: #398  
Later partition: #402

Status: structural pre-release planning. This document does not establish final chapter titles, final order, final length, manuscript acceptance, evidence clearance, or release.

## 1. Build the world before fixing the table of contents

Huey is not presently being composed toward a fixed chapter list.

The current chapter names, numbers, paths, placements and boundaries are **working editorial projections** over a larger developing body of experience, argument, memory, evidence, theology, mathematics, image and relation.

They are useful because they let work happen.

They are not authoritative because they happened to be named first.

The present composition rule is:

> **world first → continuous flow → argument cuts → late partition → final reading order**

New material therefore does not need to justify itself by fitting an existing chapter. If it belongs to Huey's world and is publicly admissible, it may first enter the pre-partition flow surface and acquire its final chapter relation later.

The book title **Huey** remains settled under the current instruction. The three-movement architecture and protected Edna close remain the current high-level structure. Within that structure, chapter identities remain deliberately plastic.

## 2. The world is larger than the argument

The world of Huey contains people, bodies, rooms, records, institutions, faith, mathematics, machines, weather, memory, color, pain, humor, birth, death, error, rescue, love, violence, care, abandonment, mercy and responsibility.

No single argument owns those things.

An experience may later support, complicate, resist or reframe several arguments. Its literary existence does not depend on how useful it is to one chapter thesis.

Experience therefore precedes argument.

A chapter may select an experience.

It may not retroactively manufacture the experience for the convenience of the argument.

The same rule applies to evidence. A scene may carry enormous literary force without becoming independent proof of a larger factual proposition. An argument may be morally urgent without becoming an adjudicated finding.

## 3. Moral simultaneity

Huey should be capable of building a world that is, at once, good and evil.

This does not mean that good and evil are indistinguishable.

It means that the narrative refuses the simplification by which a good act acquits every wrong around it, or a wrong act consumes the whole person who committed it.

The world must remain large enough to contain:

- tenderness inside damaged systems;
- cruelty inside otherwise ordinary human relations;
- courage beside fear;
- competence beside blindness;
- mercy without erasure of injury;
- accountability without reduction of a person to the worst act attributed to them;
- institutional value beside institutional failure;
- faith beside doubt;
- beauty beside violence without making violence necessary for beauty;
- care beside incapacity;
- knowledge beside uncertainty;
- love beside the possibility of grievous misunderstanding.

This is not moral vagueness.

The prose may name harm clearly.

The prose may name care clearly.

The discipline is that neither name silently becomes the entire ontology of a person.

Good does not cancel evil.

Evil does not make personhood disappear.

## 4. Baptism in the Color of Rain as mixing chamber

C08A, **Baptism in the Color of Rain**, is the current compositional center.

"Center" does not require its eventual page number to be mathematically central. It means the chapter contains an unusually dense crossing of the book's governing materials:

- birth and blood;
- recognition and misrecognition;
- race and the direction of attention;
- rescue and the limits of one's own hand;
- abandonment and accompaniment;
- speech, writing and failed channels;
- law and the search for a receiving structure;
- bodily identity and representation;
- naming and renaming without loss of personhood;
- water, rain and baptism;
- red relation beginning to become orange care.

Material written before Baptism should be allowed to acquire new meaning when it reaches this chamber.

Material written after Baptism should be allowed to leave carrying transformed but traceable motifs toward burden, institutional systems, the Pantocrator, Eucharist, mourning, Resurrection and Edna.

Do not make the center central by mechanically repeating its prose.

Let the rest of the book become increasingly legible through it.

## 5. The pre-partition flow surface

Future public working prose that is not ready for a chapter may enter:

`manuscript/flow/`

That directory is a composition surface, not a fourth movement and not an emitted manuscript by default.

A flow draft may contain:

- a complete experience;
- a remembered scene;
- a thought sequence;
- an argument seed;
- a theological meditation;
- a mathematical analogy with its limits;
- an image or recurring object;
- a transition;
- a prospective bell/intertwining;
- material that crosses several current chapter candidates.

A filename is a locator, not final literary identity.

A working heading is not a final title.

Placement in the flow directory is not evidence clearance, final admission, chapter acceptance or release.

Raw restricted evidence remains outside public Git under the existing evidence boundary.

## 6. Chapters as argument cuts through experience

A chapter should eventually be cut because an argument has become perceptible through experience.

An **argument cut** is a bounded reading projection that gathers enough lived material, reflection, counterpressure and evidence to examine one consequential question without claiming ownership of the underlying experiences.

A mature cut should generally contain:

1. **an experiential anchor** — a person, body, room, object, action or memory;
2. **an argumentative pressure** — the question the experience makes impossible to ignore;
3. **another frame** — witness, record, institution, measurement, theology, mathematics, law, reader, or another legitimate orientation;
4. **counterpressure** — uncertainty, contradiction, another interpretation, or a fact that prevents the argument from becoming propaganda;
5. **a transformation** — something the narrator/reader can no longer see in quite the same way;
6. **an exit** — often a sparse bell/intertwining rather than a conclusion that exhausts the issue.

The chapter may be lyrical.

It may be documentary.

It may be mathematical.

It may be theological.

The cut is justified by what it makes newly visible.

## 7. Working projections, not final chapter identities

The current `C01`–`C15` family and later insertion IDs remain valuable historical/staging coordinates.

They do **not** establish:

- final printed chapter numbers;
- final titles;
- final order;
- final chapter count;
- final boundaries;
- final ownership of an experience;
- a prohibition on adding new material.

A later partition may rename, reorder, split, merge, dissolve or replace chapter candidates.

When it does, lineage must remain visible.

Do not silently reinterpret an old title or number as though it had always meant the new structure.

The current IDs identify working history.

They do not imprison the future book.

## 8. Approximate 120,000-word scale horizon

The author currently expects that the finished narrative may extend to roughly **120,000 words or so**.

This is a scale estimate.

It is not:

- a quota;
- a target that prose must hit;
- a maximum;
- a minimum;
- a deadline;
- a release criterion;
- a reason to pad a scene;
- a reason to cut necessary complexity.

Counting remains useful because scale changes rhythm, reader endurance, audio duration, production and the size of an argument cut.

The count should describe the emerging book rather than govern its moral or literary content.

The supplied short Edna close remains protected from expansion merely to satisfy scale.

## 9. Partition only after enough world exists

#402 should not freeze chapter architecture until enough continuous material exists to perceive recurrent structures.

Useful cut signals include a material change in:

- scale;
- representational frame;
- provenance/time;
- dominant theological pillar;
- Four-Chamber function;
- red/orange/green motion;
- burden or agency relation;
- prosody;
- argument;
- the object around which experience is gathering.

An inherited chapter heading is not a cut signal by itself.

A word-count threshold is not a cut signal by itself.

## 10. Editorial test

When considering a new piece of content, ask first:

**Does this make the world of Huey more fully inhabited, more relationally exact, or more capable of bearing the argument without simplifying the people inside it?**

Only later ask:

**Which chapter is it?**

That order is deliberate.
