# The Human–System Contract

## Access as agency, material, and accountable relation

Authorial direction: R.A. Jacob Martone.  
Huey tracking: #1, #55, #347, #357, #364.  
Source lineage: `grwtsk/hs` founding manifesto, later author refinements, and NAV-01.

> This document imports the substantial **philosophy of access** developed in
> `grwtsk/hs` into Huey at a deeper level than interface design. It is the
> underlying contract between a human being and any system that receives,
> represents, constrains, assists, records, classifies, routes, treats, judges,
> preserves, or speaks about human action. The reader/editor is one concrete
> manifestation of that contract. The book itself is another.
>
> Huey therefore does not contain an "accessibility section" beside an otherwise
> unrelated argument. The contract is a deep grammar of the work. It permutes
> through the movements, scenes, records, institutions, technologies, evidence,
> language and acts of care already present in the book. The system changes.
> The human position changes. The medium changes. The invariant questions remain.
>
> The import is philosophical, not a copy of Hs's literal interface. Huey does
> not thereby inherit Hs's sphere, 144×144 resting mark, WebGL presentation,
> `/apple` routing example, or superseded separate intensity control.

## I. Humanity precedes the system

The first term of the contract is not interface. It is human priority.

A system may be useful, necessary, expert, lawful, efficient, technically
impressive, socially important, or built for the common good. None of those
properties makes the person an implementation detail of the system.

The person does not become a patient because a chart needs a patient field.
The person does not become a user because software needs an event source.
The person does not become a case because an institution needs a category.
The person does not become evidence because a record needs an entry.
The person does not become a demographic because a model needs a feature.
The person does not become an exception because a standard was designed around
someone else.

Systems may describe those relations. They may not reverse them.

This is not a claim that an individual's every preference defeats every shared
obligation. Humans live among other humans, and systems can legitimately protect
the agency, safety, rights and common conditions of more than one person. The
contract therefore does not abolish constraint. It makes constraint accountable.

When a system constrains a person, the system should be able to identify, at the
level appropriate to the consequence:

- what boundary is being enforced;
- whose agency or shared condition the boundary protects;
- what authority and evidence support it;
- what burden the constraint places on the person;
- what alternatives or accommodations remain;
- how the effect can be questioned, corrected, reviewed, or ended.

The system's authority is not strengthened by making those things harder to see.

"Humanity" here is not an abstraction that permits a system to sacrifice the
particular human in front of it while claiming to serve people in general. The
contract begins with concrete persons and scales outward to persons in relation.

## II. What counts as a system

For this contract, a system is any organized mechanism that can acquire durable
consequences for a person.

That includes obvious technical systems: software, models, devices, user agents,
databases, algorithms, interfaces, caches and networks. It also includes systems
whose machinery is human: professions, clinics, records, classifications,
protocols, institutions, bureaucracies, archives, legal processes, standards,
markets and organizations.

These systems are not morally or functionally equivalent. A keyboard is not a
hospital. A medical record is not a court. A color palette is not a law. The
contract does not flatten those differences.

It supplies a common boundary question:

**When a system stands between a person and an act, what must remain true so that
the system assists, constrains, records or transforms the act without silently
acquiring the person's agency?**

That question is broad enough to cross the book without pretending that every
chapter describes the same event.

## III. Access is larger than availability

Access is not satisfied because a door exists, a page loads, a form can be
submitted, a phone number is published, a clinic has appointments, a record can
technically be requested, or a control can technically be activated.

Access is the meaningful capacity to encounter a system and still be able to:

- perceive what matters;
- understand what is being asked or done;
- communicate through an effective channel;
- act without unnecessary duplication of effort;
- refuse without having refusal rewritten as incapacity or absence;
- distinguish assistance from compulsion;
- preserve authorship of one's own expression;
- know when the system has changed consequential state;
- contest, correct or qualify a representation;
- return to what was supplied before the system transformed it;
- leave, pause, or choose an alternative where the governing conditions permit.

A system can therefore be present and still be inaccessible.

A system can also be accessible in one sense and inaccessible in another. A
building may be physically reachable while its communication channel is unusable.
A document may be readable while its correction process is functionally
unreachable. A technically operable interface may still demand so much
interpretive work that the system has moved its own complexity onto the person.

Access is thus relational. It is not a badge a system awards itself.

## IV. The contract's invariants

The Hs philosophy becomes a human-system contract when its interface principles
are treated as invariants across different kinds of systems.

| Invariant | What the system must not silently do | What must remain possible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human priority | Reduce the person to the system's category or convenience | Encounter the person as the source of their own action and testimony |
| Presence is not permission | Convert availability, silence, proximity, authentication, dependence or exposure into consent | Distinguish what is merely present from what was authorized |
| Effective access | Point to a nominal pathway that cannot meaningfully receive the person | Use an effective channel or expose the actual remaining barrier |
| Burden accountability | Move system complexity downstream and call the resulting human labor a personal defect | Identify and reduce avoidable burden at the lowest-cost responsible point |
| Stable meaning | Give an act an undisclosed second consequence | Keep action, consequence and scope attributable |
| Evidentiary fidelity | Turn repetition, authority, confidence or presentation into proof | Preserve source, counterevidence, uncertainty and provenance |
| No false absence | Treat redacted, restricted, unavailable, unobserved or unrecorded as nonexistent | Preserve the correct state without leaking protected material |
| Resistance with purpose | Use a boundary as leverage until the person submits | State the reason, protect the real boundary, offer alternatives, and stop pressing |
| Correction without erasure | Repair the present by silently rewriting the past | Preserve before/after lineage, attribution and the grounds for correction |
| Equivalent agency | Make one class of body, sense, speech pattern or device carry less authority | Permit materially equivalent action through effective alternative channels |
| Scoped memory | Turn a remembered state into an expanding profile or permission | Keep retention, purpose and dissemination bounded |
| Fallback parity | Make the fallback a lower-rights version of the system | Preserve state, meaning and authority when presentation technology changes |
| Reciprocal legibility | Demand explanation from the person while leaving consequential system action opaque | Make the relevant rule, source, state and review path inspectable enough to contest the effect |
| Care without capture | Treat dependence, assistance or vulnerability as a transfer of authorship | Assist without annexing the person's voice, body, record or decision |

These invariants do not require identical procedures in every domain. They require
the same moral accounting to survive changes of domain.

## V. The system carries its own complexity

A recurring failure of systems is to move their unresolved complexity onto the
person and then describe the resulting burden as a property of that person.

The form changes:

A communication barrier produces an incomplete account. The incomplete account
produces a record dispute. The record dispute produces a correction burden. The
correction burden produces more writing. The writing is then treated as evidence
that the person is unusually demanding.

A difficult authorization model produces repeated confirmation prompts. The
prompts become "user friction." The human is then asked to learn the system's
internal distinctions rather than the system learning to preserve the human's
already expressed instruction.

An institutional uncertainty produces delay. Delay produces deterioration or
lost opportunity. The consequences are then narrated as though they arose only
from the person who could not cross the pathway.

The contract reverses this accounting.

Where the system can resolve a predictable uncertainty upstream, it should not
make the human repeatedly pay for that uncertainty downstream. The system should
carry as much of its own complexity as it responsibly can.

This does not require infinite accommodation, infinite attention, or a promise
that every desired outcome can be supplied. It requires that the cost of a system's
own architecture not be casually reclassified as a defect in the person meeting it.

## VI. Consent, refusal, and dependence

The Hs principle "presence is not permission" becomes particularly important
where a person depends on a system.

Dependence can narrow practical alternatives without creating consent.

Need can make a pathway necessary without making the pathway trustworthy.
Emergency can justify urgent action without converting every later action into
authorized continuation. A required interaction can occur without becoming
blanket agreement. Assistance can be necessary without becoming authorship.

The contract therefore keeps separate:

- necessity and consent;
- access and assent;
- participation and endorsement;
- receipt and agreement;
- stabilization and continuing authority;
- authentication and authorization;
- dependence and surrender.

A system may sometimes act without a person's preferred form of agreement under
a genuinely governing authority. When it does, it should not rewrite that
constraint as the person's voluntary instruction.

## VII. Records are systems that outlive encounters

A record is not merely storage. It is a system that can carry one encounter into
another person's future.

Once a proposition enters a durable record, later actors may encounter the record
before they encounter the person. Repetition can then look like corroboration even
when every later copy descends from the same original assertion.

The contract therefore treats provenance as an access concern.

A person must be able to remain distinguishable from a system's representation of
them. A source statement, an institutional interpretation, an inherited assertion,
an independent observation, a hypothesis, a correction and an adjudicated
conclusion must not become interchangeable merely because they share a screen.

Correction must not require destroying history. The earlier state can remain
visible as earlier state while the later correction remains visible as correction.

The system becomes more trustworthy when disagreement can be represented
faithfully without requiring one side to disappear.

## VIII. Evidence is a boundary on system authority

Systems accumulate authority easily. A title, credential, database field,
published standard, confident model output, institutional letter, polished
visualization or repeated note can appear more settled than the evidence beneath
it.

The contract does not deny expertise or institutional knowledge. It denies the
automatic conversion of status into proof.

A system making a consequential claim should preserve enough of the path from
claim to grounds that the claim can be examined at the appropriate level.

This applies in both directions. Personal testimony is not made false because it
is personal. Institutional documentation is not made true in every respect because
it is institutional. A model's uncertainty is not evidence that all competing
accounts deserve equal weight. A person's certainty is not by itself proof.

The relevant distinction is not person versus system.

It is assertion, observation, evidence, inference, contradiction, uncertainty,
proof where proof is possible, and adjudication where adjudication belongs.

Access to truth requires access to those distinctions.

## IX. Resistance protects a boundary; it does not acquire the person

A system sometimes has to resist.

A color relation approaches unreadability. A requested disclosure exceeds
permission. A proposed edit would silently alter another source. A claim outruns
its evidence. A navigation destination is ambiguous. A procedure would violate a
governing constraint.

Resistance is legitimate when it protects something the contract itself requires.

But resistance becomes domination when the system converts "I cannot do this
without crossing a boundary" into "I will continue pressing until you want what
I want."

A responsible system states the reason at the level the person needs, preserves
the permitted part of the person's action, offers a meaningful alternative where
one exists, and stops repeating the objection once the boundary has been made
clear.

The system can retain its own record of the disagreement without making the human
defeat the system in a contest of endurance.

## X. Care is not ownership

Hs names sanctuary, care and communion. The human-system contract makes care a
test of authority.

Care can involve expertise, dependence, asymmetry, urgency and trust. Those facts
make care more powerful. They do not make the cared-for person less authoritative
over their own voice.

A caring system should be able to assist a person whose capacity to use the
system's preferred channel is limited without treating the channel limitation as
a reduction in mind, credibility, authorship or personhood.

Support can increase a person's agency. It must not quietly transfer the person's
agency to the supporter.

The system should not demand that a person become expert in the system merely to
be treated as fully human inside it.

## XI. The permutation grammar of Huey

Huey uses this contract as a literary invariant.

"Permutation" here is a literary and editorial operation, not a claim that human
relations form a mathematical permutation group. The book changes the coordinates
of the relation while preserving the questions that make the relation legible.

A scene, argument or record can be described provisionally as:

`<human, system, channel, authority, burden, record, evidence, remedy>`

The book may change any coordinate:

- scale: tool → professional → department → institution → network;
- channel: body → speech → writing → record → code → publication;
- time: encounter → documentation → inheritance → correction → memory;
- authority: suggestion → request → requirement → constraint → adjudication;
- representation: sensation → statement → measurement → classification → model;
- scope: private → interpersonal → clinical → institutional → public;
- locality: immediate → stored → cached → distributed;
- burden: system-borne → shared → shifted onto the human;
- remedy: pause → clarification → accommodation → correction → review → lawful
  external process.

The invariant is not that every permutation has the same moral weight. It is that
the book keeps asking what happens to human agency, access, burden, evidence,
consent and correction as the system changes.

This is how the contract **permutes the book**.

The reader should be able to feel the same underlying relation before the book
names it abstractly, encounter it again when its scale changes, and recognize it
at the end without receiving a lecture that explains the ending away.

## XII. Permutation is not repetition

The contract should recur as structure, not as an aphorism pasted into every
chapter.

A chapter need not say "presence is not permission" to stage a relation in which
presence has been mistaken for permission. It need not define provenance to show
what happens when one statement is copied until it appears independently
corroborated. It need not explain density to let the reader feel a page, encounter
or institution become too demanding.

The editorial task is therefore to preserve the invariant while changing the
surface.

A useful permutation changes at least one of:

- who holds formal authority;
- what medium carries the human;
- what the system knows or thinks it knows;
- what the system asks the human to do;
- where the burden lands;
- what becomes durable;
- how correction becomes possible;
- what care looks like at that scale.

If only the nouns change, the book is repeating itself.

If the relation changes so completely that the human-system contract disappears,
the book loses its deep grammar.

## XIII. The three movements carry different scales of the same contract

The existing three-movement architecture remains unchanged.

### Preamble — the human before the machinery

The Preamble should establish the person before the system has finished naming
the person.

Its dominant scale is close: body, room, speech, witness, encounter, note,
immediate authority. The philosophical work is concrete before it is theoretical.

The reader learns the contract through consequences: what is heard, what is not,
what enters a record, what cannot be interrupted, what survives as someone else's
description.

The Preamble asks the first form of the question:

**What happens when a system acquires durable power over a human account before
it has adequately received the human?**

### Interlude — propagation through systems

The Interlude permits the relation to expand.

The same human action can move through records, referrals, institutions,
classifications, professional roles, evidence systems, language, technology,
ethics, law, family, faith and public consequence where those domains are already
present in the manuscript.

The system is no longer one room. It becomes a chain.

The book can therefore examine not only a harmful act but the propagation of
burden: how one unresolved relation enters another system; how copied information
acquires apparent weight; how a failed channel produces more compensatory work;
how care becomes administration; how administration becomes a record; how a
record changes future care.

The Interlude asks the scaled question:

**When responsibility is distributed, does human agency become better protected,
or merely harder to locate?**

The contract requires the book to follow the consequence across the boundaries
rather than letting every system treat the previous system's output as nature.

### Excursion: Edna — return to the human scale

The closing excursion should not become a summary of the contract.

It returns scale to the child, the room, color, relation, question and response.
The protected wording remains protected under PR-01/#380; this contract does not
preview, paraphrase or explain it.

The philosophical function is more important than exposition: after the book has
shown increasingly elaborate systems, the ending returns to an encounter in which
a human question can simply be received.

The contract is not finally stated.

It is embodied.

## XIV. Chapter-scale permutation review

During drafting and the full-book developmental pass, each chapter should be read
for its position in the permutation rather than forced to restate the entire
philosophy.

For an existing chapter, ask:

1. Who is the concrete human before classification?
2. What system, role, record, rule, tool or institution mediates the scene?
3. What channel carries the human into that system?
4. What does the system treat as permission, evidence, absence or authority?
5. Which burden belongs to the underlying problem, and which burden is generated
   by the system itself?
6. What becomes durable after the encounter?
7. Can the human correct, refuse, qualify, pause, return, or obtain another route?
8. Does assistance preserve authorship, or acquire it?
9. What previous permutation does this chapter transform rather than merely
   repeat?
10. What later permutation does the chapter make possible without prematurely
    explaining it?

These are editorial questions. They are not headings to insert into the narrative.

## XV. The contract must operate on Huey itself

A book arguing that systems must remain accountable to humans cannot exempt its
own system of production.

Huey therefore has to practice the contract in its source, editing, evidence,
reader and publication architecture.

The manuscript must not disappear because publication has not admitted it.
Unavailable material must not be called nonexistent. A local edit must not become
publication by accident. A copied claim must not become independent
corroboration. A protected source must not be exposed to make the public apparatus
look complete. A model must not invent missing prose to preserve narrative flow.
A formatting operation must not alter literary identity. A software check must
not become a truth verdict. An author's instruction must not be downgraded into a
default merely because a system has a preferred workflow.

The book's infrastructure is therefore part of the philosophical demonstration.

If Huey's production system violates the contract while its prose praises the
contract, the contradiction belongs to Huey.

## XVI. Interface specialization

The reader/editor is one system governed by the contract above. The remaining
sections specialize the same invariants for presentation and interaction.

## 1. The interface belongs to the person using it

Hs begins with a human behind a user agent. Huey adopts that boundary.

The interface does not acquire authority merely because it renders the book,
implements an accessibility standard, predicts a preference, observes a repeated
choice, knows a location, has a cache, or has enough computation to make an
inference. Standards, models, institutions, design systems and population
averages may constrain or inform the available presentation. They do not become
the person's agent.

A user-agent instruction is not a default. It is an instruction.

For Huey this means that presentation state, editing state, navigation state,
source state, publication state, and evidence state remain distinguishable.
Assistance may help the person reach an intended act; it may not silently turn
that act into a different one.

The governing test is not engagement. It is the amount of **unwanted work**
between a person and a chosen act.

## 2. Presence is not permission

Hs states the principle directly: presence is not permission.

Huey therefore must not infer permission or intent merely from:

- opening the page;
- hovering, dwelling, scrolling, pausing or returning;
- selecting text without committing a change;
- a cached state or earlier local draft;
- a browser or device capability;
- model confidence;
- the visibility of a control;
- an authenticated identity by itself;
- the existence of additional material that could be shown.

No idle animation, unsolicited personalization, surprise navigation, automatic
publication, evidence mutation, source admission, or permission expansion follows
from those signals.

The interface may make a capability available without demanding that the person
exercise it. Accessibility does not require a configuration ceremony before the
book can be read.

## 3. Silence is an access property, not absence

Hs distinguishes a quiet interface from an inaccessible one. Huey carries that
distinction forward.

A page can remain visually restrained while its purpose, state and available
actions remain programmatically intelligible. Conversely, adding explanation,
badges, helper copy or persistent controls can increase access cost even when each
individual element is technically accessible.

Silence therefore means:

- do not demand attention before the person asks for something;
- do not add explanatory chrome merely to prove a capability exists;
- do not confuse visual minimalism with semantic disappearance;
- do not hide necessary state from assistive technology;
- do not require sight, pointer precision, motion perception or memorized gesture
  knowledge to discover the same essential operation.

Huey's minimal toolbar, native selection/caret and semantic document structure
should remain sufficient without an additional tutorial layer becoming a
prerequisite for use.

## 4. Accessibility belongs to what the material can become

The strongest Hs accessibility rule is structural:

> Do not offer an inaccessible state and call the opportunity to select it agency.

Huey adopts that rule as an implementation constraint.

Accessibility is not a warning displayed after a failure, a separate "accessible
mode", or a cleanup pass applied after arbitrary presentation choices have already
been admitted. The reachable presentation space itself must be constrained so
that the person's operation cannot produce a state that makes the instrument or
the information unusable.

This applies to more than color.

A presentation operation must not be admitted when it would make essential text
unreadable, controls inoperable, focus indiscernible, content unreachable,
navigation ambiguous, or an edit impossible to recover. Where a requested
relation approaches such a boundary, the rendering may compress, normalize,
exchange roles, stage content, or otherwise preserve the requested direction
without crossing into failure.

The constraint belongs to the material. The person should encounter a usable
surface, not a reprimand for having moved a control "too far."

## 5. Equivalent agency must not become a dexterity test

Hs requires equivalent keyboard, assistive-technology and other authorized inputs
to carry the same agency as pointer manipulation. NAV-01 adds a specific caution:
a keyboard alternative alone does not remove every single-pointer dexterity
barrier.

Huey therefore treats equivalent operation as a semantic requirement, not an
afterthought.

For every essential operation, test the capability rather than the exact gesture:

- selection and caret placement;
- opening and dismissing formatting controls;
- changing document or local presentation;
- activating a link;
- distinguishing editing from following;
- navigating between reading units;
- exposing evidence/source context;
- restoring or abandoning a local edit.

No essential operation should require a drag, hover, timed gesture, rapid speech,
fine motor precision, color discrimination, animation perception, or a second
input that merely repeats the first intention.

Equivalent paths may differ physically. They must not differ in authority.

## 6. Density is the combined burden of design and content

Hs defines density as the whole demand placed on the person, not merely spatial
compactness. Huey imports that definition.

The burden includes:

- how many choices appear simultaneously;
- how much qualification must be held at once;
- how much explanatory structure is exposed;
- how many visual regions compete for attention;
- how much searching is required to find the next relevant action;
- how much context must be reconstructed before a claim is intelligible;
- how much interface must be understood before the book itself can be read.

A visually sparse interface can still be dense. A hidden control can impose more
burden than a visible one. A short sentence can carry more interpretive demand
than a large image.

Reducing density may stage information, defer optional detail, alter grouping or
permit a slower route. It must not delete material qualifications, sever a claim
from its provenance, convert uncertainty into certainty, or conceal that more
context exists.

Increasing density is not permission to add decoration.

Density is a budget of demand, not a measurement of the reader's mind.

## 7. Stable meanings make access trustworthy

Hs requires that learned movement/consequence relationships remain stable.
Huey's equivalent rule is that a control, gesture, link or edit must not acquire
an undisclosed second effect.

Examples:

- changing font size does not silently admit more content;
- changing document color does not publish a local edit;
- selecting text does not navigate away;
- autosave does not become submission;
- opening evidence does not alter the claim;
- following a link does not silently rewrite the link text;
- a local draft does not become a source amendment;
- an accessibility preference does not authorize unrelated personalization.

The person should not have to reverse-engineer the interface's hidden coupling.

The effect of an operation must remain attributable and inspectable.

## 8. Resistance must have a reason and an end

Hs develops "resistance" as a way for an interface or model to preserve a real
boundary without converting that boundary into domination.

Huey adopts the same distinction.

Resistance is appropriate when a requested state would:

- violate an accessibility invariant;
- obscure a material qualification;
- break source/provenance identity;
- cross a disclosure or permission boundary;
- conflate local expression with publication;
- present an unsupported proposition as established;
- create a navigation ambiguity that the system cannot responsibly resolve.

Resistance should identify the concrete reason and, where possible, offer a usable
alternative. It must preserve the person's permitted edit or local rendering when
that can be done without falsifying source/evidence state.

Resistance must not become repetition until submission. Once the person has
declined an advisory and the requested local action remains permitted, the system
should yield. The objection may remain inspectable without continuing to interrupt.

A boundary can remain firm without making the human win a contest against the
software.

## 9. Color is one materialization of the access philosophy

Huey's color system under #357 is a direct case of the Hs material-boundary rule.

The achromatic points are:

- `#1e1e1e`;
- paper `#ffffff`;
- warm white veil `rgba(255, 254, 253, .87)` over `#ffffff`.

Chromatic choice is restricted to the accessible relational system derived from
Edna's formula: **orange, green and hue**. The intermediate gamut should remain
within the intended human skin/olive continuum rather than becoming an arbitrary
accent rainbow.

The important philosophical requirement is not merely that each final swatch
passes a contrast test. The user should not be led through unreadable intermediate
states or offered a color relation that can only be repaired after selection.

Therefore:

- interpolate in a perceptually appropriate relational space;
- evaluate the rendered foreground/background pair;
- compress or resist before contrast fails;
- let light/dark exchange foreground/background roles rather than crossfade
  through sameness;
- allow forced-colors/high-contrast user-agent modes to override authored color;
- never make hue the only carrier of meaning;
- preserve the person's chosen relation where possible rather than replacing it
  with an unrelated "safe" theme.

The person keeps the relation. The presentation may change orientation.

## 10. Typography, measure and spacing obey the same rule

Typography is not decoration added after access is solved.

Local and document typography must remain relational so that global changes
preserve hierarchy rather than freezing inaccessible absolute values. Size,
measure, line-height, weight, tracking and type role must be normalized against
the document basis and current viewport/user-agent conditions.

A local style request must not:

- make text effectively disappear;
- collapse readable line-height or measure;
- make focus/caret/selection impossible to perceive;
- require horizontal scrolling for ordinary reflowed prose without a material
  reason;
- compound nested relative spans into an unusable extreme;
- rewrite lexical identity merely to achieve presentation.

Presentation may adapt. Inscription remains separately accountable.

## 11. Editing is access to expression; evidence remains accountable

Hs says that the person may edit presented text, but editing a claim does not make
the edited claim demonstrated. Huey's existing local-editor/source boundary is
therefore part of access, not merely provenance infrastructure.

The person must be able to alter local wording, organization and presentation
without the interface pretending that the canonical source, evidence ledger or
publication state changed with it.

Maintain visible distinctions among:

- source inscription;
- local draft;
- proposed correction;
- admitted/publication wording;
- evidence or proof attached to a claim;
- personal rendering or annotation.

Evidence is not protected from challenge. It is protected from silent
substitution.

A lower-density rendering may stage the evidence. It may not make the evidence
disappear or imply that no qualification exists.

## 12. Editing and following are both real capabilities

NAV-01 makes the page itself an input rather than requiring the human to restate
an intention in a second control. Huey should inherit the access principle without
copying Hs's URL-routing implementation.

Text may be both editable and navigable. Those capabilities must be visibly and
programmatically distinguishable without relying only on color.

Do not turn the same undifferentiated action into both caret placement and
navigation.

Selection, incomplete typing, IME composition, dictation correction, autosave and
local formatting must not unexpectedly navigate away. Following a link should be
an intentional activation. A local preview may refine what is shown without
discarding the active edit.

The interface's internal organization must not require the person to express the
same intention twice.

## 13. Missing, restricted and redacted are not "nothing"

Hs's author refinement on redaction rejects the false claim that no information
exists merely because the current user agent may not receive it.

Huey's inventory/traversal already represents unavailable and partial material.
The access philosophy makes that distinction normative.

Where material is unavailable, restricted, withheld, pending, offline, not
admitted, or not yet examined, the system must preserve the correct state rather
than collapse it into absence or fabricate substitute content.

Redaction may conceal content. It must not falsify existence.

A placeholder should communicate only what the current permission/source state
actually supports. It should not leak restricted content in order to prove that
something is present.

## 14. Locality may assist; it may not classify the person

Hs allows locality and caches to improve delivery and orientation while refusing
to treat proximity, popularity or accumulated use as authority over the person.

Huey adopts the same limit.

Cached or local information may reduce latency, preserve a permitted draft, or
help return to a chosen place. It does not establish:

- truth;
- preference;
- identity;
- ability;
- belief;
- permission to publish;
- permission to profile;
- permission to disclose a precise location;
- permission to expand the scope of an earlier grant.

A suggestion does not silently become a preference. A remembered state does not
silently become consent.

The cache may assist. It may not decide.

## 15. User-agent authority survives fallback and adaptation

A particular rendering technology is never the source of legitimacy.

Huey's accessible contract must survive:

- forced-colors/high-contrast modes;
- reduced motion;
- zoom/reflow;
- keyboard-only use;
- touch and single-pointer use;
- screen readers and other assistive technology;
- unavailable scripts or richer visual effects;
- narrow or enlarged viewports.

A fallback may simplify presentation. It must not replace the person's state with
different semantics, different permissions, or a reduced authority model.

The metaphor does not outrank the person.

## 16. Rigor and gentleness are not opposites

Access does not require euphemism, false balance or the deletion of difficult
material.

Hs's later NAV-01/RG-01 work makes a useful distinction: gentleness changes the
manner of address, not which identities, counterevidence, limitations or
uncertainties survive it.

For Huey this means that an accessible rendering may change pacing, grouping,
density and presentation while retaining the underlying record distinctions
required by RF-01 and the evidence model.

The interface should not make the person perform additional investigative work
because the software replaced a concrete limitation with vague caution. Where a
boundary or uncertainty matters, name the actual boundary or uncertainty.

Accessibility must not become another way to conceal what the book is saying.

## 17. The human remains at every boundary

Huey should be able to account for why each boundary exists.

- A color boundary protects usable distinction.
- A typography boundary protects readability and operation.
- A density boundary protects the person's chosen level of demand.
- An evidence boundary protects assertion from silent promotion into proof.
- A source boundary protects provenance and rights.
- A permission boundary protects the human represented by the user agent.
- A publication boundary protects local expression from unintended dissemination.

None of these boundaries exists to make the system more important.

The interface must not convert assistance into authority, convenience into
consent, prediction into instruction, or implementation complexity into work the
person must perform.

There is a human behind the user agent.

## 18. Huey-specific acceptance obligations

The reader/editor work should be rejected when an implementation:

1. offers an authored presentation state that fails the applicable accessibility
   invariant and merely warns afterward;
2. makes an essential operation depend on color, motion, fine dragging, rapid
   input, or a pointer-only path;
3. creates a keyboard path but leaves an otherwise avoidable single-pointer
   dexterity barrier;
4. changes the meaning of a control or gesture based on hidden prediction,
   popularity or experiment;
5. treats hover, dwell, visit, cache presence, silence or model confidence as
   permission;
6. conflates local editing, navigation, autosave, publication or evidence
   mutation;
7. lowers density by deleting material qualifications or source/evidence routes;
8. claims unavailable, restricted, redacted or unadmitted material does not
   exist;
9. converts forced-colors/reduced-motion/fallback rendering into a loss of state
   or agency;
10. requires the person to repeat an already expressed intention in an additional
    configuration surface without a real independent choice;
11. repeatedly pressures the person to accept an advisory after a permitted local
    choice has been made;
12. uses accessibility adaptation to rewrite inscription, source identity,
    evidentiary status or RF-01 distinctions;
13. encodes meaning only through the Edna color relation or any other visual
    channel;
14. silently turns a remembered state or local edit into a preference, profile,
    shared contribution or permission expansion.

Tests should cover the capability and the boundary, not merely the presence of a
particular widget.

and does not replace domain-specific factual, legal, clinical, ethical or
implementation review.

Its interface specialization governs #357/#364. Its literary permutation governs
the developmental work under #55 and the broader production charter under #1.

The deepest invariant is:

**access is the condition under which a human can act, refuse, understand,
communicate, correct, and remain attributable to themself without the system
silently acquiring the human's agency.**

The corresponding obligation on systems is:

**carry your own complexity; expose consequential boundaries; preserve the human's
authorship; distinguish permission from presence and proof from authority; and
remain corrigible where your representation can change a human life.**
