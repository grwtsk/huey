# Lexical and conceptual geometry

**LG-01 · First research edition · race / is / -ism / -ist / racism / racist**

Work: [#102](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/102). Continuing research: [#103](https://github.com/grwtsk/huey/issues/103). Editorial method: [RF-01](../../planning/relational-framing.md).

This atlas records meanings, constructions, historical connections, disagreements and relations among words, people, practices and institutions. It is public-source research, not the private manuscript or an application of a classification to its participants. Its sources are not forced into either a required label or a required denial.

The edition contains **41 source records, 29 senses, 25 typed relations, eight comparisons and thirteen continuing research areas**. The [source register](sources.md) identifies versions and the actual scope retrieved. [atlas.json](atlas.json) separates each attributed source summary from editorial synthesis. [coverage.md](coverage.md) makes unfinished primary-text, historical, multilingual and jurisdictional work explicit. All requested dimensions are represented; this is not a claim that every pertinent authority or historical occurrence has been exhausted.

## 1. Authority is itself a relation

A dictionary is evidence of lexical treatment; a grammar describes constructions; a philosopher may propose or contest a concept; a community speaks concerning its identity; a scientific statement evaluates a specified scientific question. A treaty, declaration, statute and policy glossary have different reach. None becomes an authority for everything merely by being authoritative somewhere.

The organization below is editorial synthesis. Source IDs make its dependencies inspectable. They do not imply that the cited authors jointly endorsed the synthesis. An abstract remains an abstract, a translation remains a particular translation, and a bibliographic record does not count as a read argument. Four important sources are therefore listed as metadata-only targets, not used to support substantive propositions.

The geometry has distinguishable layers:

```text
form and word history <-> construction and predicate scope
          |                          |
identity and naming <-> speaker, referent and occasion
          |                          |
action / condition / practice <-> reception and consequences
          |                          |
institutional arrangement <-> normative or legal classification
```

The arrows indicate possible research relations, not established causal paths or shared etymologies. For each edge, record its source and status. An unknown edge is not a reason to delete its endpoints.

## 2. Race: motion, descent, human grouping and belonging

The descent/classification and movement/contest histories of English *race* are distinct in Merriam-Webster. The former passes through French and Italian; the latter has a Germanic history. Broad humankind, lineage, kind, current and course senses make a single undifferentiated definition inadequate. A separate ginger-root homograph is recorded by Dictionary.com. Literary connections among these uses do not depend on inventing a common root. [L01](sources.md#l01), [L08](sources.md#l08)

The further ancestry of Italian *razza* is contested even among the inspected dictionaries. The Académie tentatively connects it to Latin *generatio* through apheresis; Treccani proposes Old French *haraz*, associated with horse breeding, through resegmentation. Both remain attributed proposals. The map records disagreement rather than declaring an institutional winner. [L09](sources.md#l09), [L10](sources.md#l10)

Lexical history is not current scientific validation. Historical racial classifications, animal-breeding senses and broader collective uses must retain their context. Nor does a homograph license treating a contemporary group as a different biological species. The task is to preserve distinctions that the word has carried, not turn every old sense into an instruction for present use.

The referents also differ: descent, ancestry, nationality, citizenship, ethnicity, peoplehood, perceived bodily characteristics, self-description and administrative classification can intersect without being synonyms. Du Bois’s 1897 address is a primary historical example of moving beyond physical criteria toward common history, traditions and striving, while addressing American and Black belonging together. Its period claims are not silently modernized. [H01](sources.md#h01)

AABA distinguishes the social reality of race from discrete biological racial partitions. NASEM’s selected recommendations distinguish population descriptors, genetic ancestry, self-identification and relevant exposures. OMB’s 2024 principles describe statistical categories as sociopolitical, not biological or genetic. These sources answer different questions; none requires racial identity to disappear from social inquiry. [S01](sources.md#s01), [S02](sources.md#s02), [S03](sources.md#s03)

An occurrence of *race* therefore needs both a referent and an identifying relation: who names whom, by which convention, at which time, and for which purpose? The person, the self-description and another party’s categorization may all belong in the record. They must not be silently collapsed into one field.

## 3. Is: inflection, predication, identification and existence

In standard English, *is* is the third-person singular present indicative of *be*. Merriam-Webster also marks dialectal distributions and distinguishes unrelated prefix/abbreviation entries. Its cognates include German *ist*, Latin *est* and Greek *esti*. That connection does not make the English suffix *-ist* a form of the copula. [L02](sources.md#l02)

Cambridge distinguishes main and auxiliary uses, temporary and continuing descriptions, and several complement types. Aristotle’s *On Interpretation*, in the inspected Edghill translation, distinguishes isolated terms from the combinations that can assert something. The sentence, not merely the letters *is*, is the relevant unit of analysis. [G01](sources.md#g01), [G02](sources.md#g02), [P01](sources.md#p01)

These are synthetic examples, not case evidence:

| Construction | Example | Scope to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Property predication | The document is incomplete. | A property of the document, not identity with incompleteness itself. |
| Identification | The depositor is the author. | On an identifying reading, two descriptions pick out one person. |
| Existence | There is an account. | Existence does not yet establish accuracy or acceptance. |
| Situation or location | The account is in the archive. | A relation to a location, not the account’s complete nature. |
| Progressive auxiliary | The reviewer is reading. | An unfolding action, with tense and aspect. |
| Passive auxiliary | The entry is being omitted. | An operation described without naming its agent. |
| Ambiguous event/state | The entry is excluded. | Context determines whether this describes an event, a state or a result. |

Identification, predication and existence are related analytical categories, not interchangeable readings. The table is editorial analysis supported by the grammar sources, not a claim that every philosopher divides the copula identically. Frege’s original argument remains a metadata-level target rather than being reconstructed from reputation. [P05](sources.md#p05)

A further distinction concerns the work a concept is asked to do. Haslanger separates conceptual, descriptive and analytical inquiry and proposes her own revisionary account. Its particular aim must remain attributed; it does not automatically replace the book’s descriptive purpose. [P02](sources.md#p02)

The grammatical predicate also has limits of scope. *This remark is racist*, *this practice is racist* and *this person is a racist* do not have the same subject or construction. Neither an adjective nor a noun automatically states an immutable, exhaustive essence. Time, quantification, modality, negation and context matter. This is a distinction about what has been said, not a way to supply or withhold evidence for it.

## 4. -ism: action and condition, not doctrine alone

The inspected dictionaries give *-ism* a range that includes **action, process, practice, behavior, condition, state, property, characteristic and doctrine**, with discrimination or prejudice in relevant formations. Collins explicitly includes state of being. The independent noun *ism* and the productive suffix family must also be distinguished. [L03](sources.md#l03), [L07](sources.md#l07)

This matters to the relationship the user has asked to examine. A named *-ism* need not mean that someone consciously professes a doctrine. Depending on the actual word and its use, the description can concern a practice, a condition or a continuing arrangement. It is legitimate to examine those connections between being and doing.

That semantic relation does not require a shared etymology. The suffix’s recorded route through French and Latin to Greek formations is different from the history of the English verb *is*. Saying so distinguishes two kinds of connection; it does not invalidate a conceptual or literary relation between the words. [L02](sources.md#l02), [L03](sources.md#l03)

Nor is suffix substitution an automatic definition machine. A formed word acquires uses in particular languages, periods and practices. Morphology opens a field of possible relations; attested usage specifies which relations a particular occurrence carries.

## 5. -ist and racist: person, participation and adjective

The *-ist* family includes the doer, practitioner, specialist and adherent, as well as adjectival association. *Racist* has both noun and adjective entries. Its adjective can apply beyond an individual belief to conduct or systemic policy. [L04](sources.md#l04), [L06](sources.md#l06)

A person’s relation to an act, an act’s relation to a practice, and a practice’s relation to an institution are distinct edges. An account should neither automatically identify them nor sever them in advance. A nominal characterization may carry broader implications than a bounded act-description, but that difference has to be read in context, not imposed by suffix alone.

Words are also addressed and received. Austin’s and Cassin’s available abstracts offer different conceptual routes into the relation between saying something and doing something through speech. Here they support a limited comparison, not a claim that declaring a proposition makes it true. Their complete theories and translation histories remain research work. [P03](sources.md#p03), [P04](sources.md#p04)

## 6. Racism: multiple objects of analysis

Merriam-Webster records belief, behavior and systemic senses. Even its historical dating needs care: the First Known Use field gives 1886 while the usage discussion reports no apparent pre-twentieth-century examples. This edition records the inconsistency and keeps original-attestation research open. [L05](sources.md#l05)

The authorities do not simply provide interchangeable wordings for one settled object:

| Account | Unit or emphasis in the inspected source | Reading scope |
|---|---|---|
| ECRI | Belief justifying contempt/superiority; separate entries for discriminatory operations and structures. | Direct glossary. |
| Jones | Institutionalized, personally mediated and internalized relations, including omissions and unintentional conduct. | Direct interview; original article abstract separately. |
| Bonilla-Silva | Racialized social systems. | Author-institution abstract. |
| Quijano | Racial/ethnic classification in colonial power. | Original Spanish abstract. |
| Appiah | Ideology, racialism and morally impermissible attitudes as differentiated concepts. | Indexed original abstract. |
| Webster | Moral implications of racial essentialism and stereotyping for agency. | Indexed abstract and selected opening. |
| Shelby | Empirical and normative social criticism beyond individual moralism. | Direct 2014 abstract; 2002 work remains metadata. |
| Scott | Truth-distorting racial attitudes and personal racism, distinguished from non-personal entities. | Selected original text. |

Sources: [N04](sources.md#n04), [M01](sources.md#m01), [M02](sources.md#m02), [H02–H10](sources.md#h02).

A belief-centered glossary entry cannot be quoted as though its author denies structures described elsewhere in the same glossary. Conversely, a framework that includes omissions does not make every omission the same operation. The source’s criteria, scale and object must travel with its name.

Appiah and Webster supply an actual disagreement rather than an invented consensus. Webster accepts central ideology/oppression claims while disputing the moral separation of false racial essentialism. That debate must not be paraphrased into a prohibition on recognizing socially real identity. Scott offers a different epistemic account centered on truth-distorting attitudes; it is not a universal resolution of the debate. [H04](sources.md#h04), [H05](sources.md#h05), [H10](sources.md#h10)

Garcia’s 1996 and Shelby’s 2002 original arguments remain retrieval targets. Their existence and bibliographic metadata are recorded, but neither is supplied through an opponent’s précis. Omi and Winant’s publisher synopsis is likewise an orientation, not a read book. [H07](sources.md#h07), [H08](sources.md#h08), [H09](sources.md#h09)

The comparative record asks: what is classified, what relation or operation is decisive, what evidence is relevant, and what explanatory purpose the definition serves? It does not vote among authorities or select a definition to predetermine an individual history.

## 7. Identity, recognition and reception as a knower

Fricker’s available abstract identifies testimonial and hermeneutical injustice. Dotson’s abstract examines failures of reception through pernicious ignorance in a relation of communicative dependence. These are pertinent to the geometry of who enters an account as a knower. They remain conceptual resources at the accessed depth, not automatic classifications of particular interactions. Crenshaw’s original journal record is located; the full paper has not been inspected in this retry. [E01](sources.md#e01), [E02](sources.md#e02), [E03](sources.md#e03)

The atlas’s own reading protocol distinguishes the person, identity, account offered, opportunity to observe, reception, selection, representation, transmission and correction. A missing record of reception is not a missing person. Receiving a statement, understanding it, accepting it and preserving it are different relationships. Unknown links are recorded as unknown; supplied identity is not made conditional on an eventual classification.

This protocol applies symmetrically to the author, witness, clinician, institution, scholar and editor. Different access and expertise may matter to specific propositions. They do not give one participant exclusive ownership of truth. Preserved disagreement differs from removal of a contribution, and an institutional document is itself an attributable contribution.

## 8. Normative and legal geometry

UNESCO’s 1978 declaration joins equal dignity with recognition of difference and cultural identity and includes structural/institutional practices in its account. ICERD Article 1 defines *racial discrimination* through specified operations, grounds, purpose or effect and equal rights, with adjacent qualifications that must not be omitted. These are related but different instruments and terms. [N01](sources.md#n01), [N02](sources.md#n02)

McDougall’s UN note is named expert commentary on ICERD, not the treaty itself. UNDRIP’s provisions concerning forced assimilation, culture, names, community and membership have a specific collective-rights context. They cannot be reduced to a generic administrative race field. [N03](sources.md#n03), [N05](sources.md#n05)

Title VII is a U.S. employment statute with different treatment, disparate-impact and motivating-factor provisions. Its criteria cannot be imported into another setting without a jurisdictional and temporal analysis. A legal standard does not monopolize the ordinary word, and ordinary usage does not by itself satisfy a particular legal cause of action. [N06](sources.md#n06)

**Recognition, classification, exclusion, purpose, effect, participation and authority** can therefore be compared without treating the frameworks as synonyms. A definition’s normative function and the evidence about an actual arrangement remain separately inspectable.

## 9. Recording an occurrence without replacing it with a label

The structured atlas supplies a context-field vocabulary, not a populated case ledger. It includes the exact expression, source/speaker, version/locator, time, language, construction, referent, predicate scope, identifying authority, identities supplied, operation, included/omitted participants, evidence status, uptake, jurisdiction, alternatives, unknowns and revision lineage.

For example, a synthetic record could distinguish an administrative category, a contributor’s self-description, an available form that retains one but not the other, and an unestablished reason for the difference. The relational description does not require inventing a private motive. Nor does it permit filling the unknown reason with a predetermined label.

When a classification is offered, record **who classifies what, using which sense and authority**. Do not replace the underlying arrangement with that latest name. An original source keeps its language and date; a later correction can change the current interpretation without pretending the earlier words were different. RF-01 protects this distinction without banning the vocabulary under study.

The atlas is a research map: forms have histories, constructions have scopes, identities have speakers and communities, and practices have participants and effects. Its edges are revisable. No one suffix, copula or institutional definition is required to carry the whole account.

## 10. Verification and extension

From a checkout using Python 3.11 or later:

```sh
python3 scripts/lexical_geometry.py validate
python3 scripts/lexical_geometry.py check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_lexical_geometry.py' -v
```

`render` prints generated source/coverage pages as JSON; it does not write files. The checker validates bounded inputs, access-declaration agreement, references, term coverage and designated conceptual distinctions. It has no network, permission, classification, editing or publication function.

The observations file is an agent-recorded retrieval declaration, not independent authentication. Two matching declarations can be falsified together; a test explicitly demonstrates that limit. No passing test proves meaning, source truth, exhaustive coverage or human acceptance. The [verification and recovery note](verification.md) records actual commands and corrections to the unfinished candidate.

The [thirteen-area research register](coverage.md) continues historical attestation, original-language morphology, fuller philosophy and social theory, community-authored and multilingual work, epistemic/intersectional texts, jurisdiction, scientific depth and independent review. These are active research omissions, not evidence that the unrepresented traditions have nothing to say. They do not prevent use of the source-grounded material already available.
